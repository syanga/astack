# Hook formulas

Each formula is a headline shape, with an example for a made-up coffee roaster and the formula's trap, the most common way it fails.

Two rules cover every formula:
- **Say the number.** When the headline states an amount, a time, or a price, use the number the cited page gives. "Fast" and "a lot" are not numbers.
- **Make it this brand's.** If the headline would work for any other brand in the niche, it isn't this brand's hook. Put in the thing only this brand can say.

| Formula | Headline shape | Example | Trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stop | Stop {common habit}. | STOP BUYING BEANS ROASTED IN MAY | A habit nobody has. The reader has to recognize it in themselves. |
| Permission | You don't need {thing everyone says you need}. | YOU DON'T NEED A $600 GRINDER | Taking away the blocker and offering nothing in its place. The sub-line gives the replacement. |
| Wrong way | {Doing it wrong}? Not your fault. | BREWING TOO HOT? NOT YOUR FAULT | Blaming the reader. Without the absolution, it reads as an insult. |
| Situation | {Specific situation}? | OUT OF BEANS ON A MONDAY? | A situation so broad it qualifies nobody. "Want better coffee?" is not a situation. |
| Callout | {Specific group}: {what this is}. | POUR-OVER PEOPLE: THIS IS YOUR ROAST | A group too large to be a callout. "Coffee lovers" is not a group. |
| Objection | "{Objection, in the customer's words}" | "SUBSCRIPTIONS ALWAYS PILE UP" | Inventing the objection. Take it from pasted ads, reviews, or the site's FAQ. |
| Question | {Question as customers type it}? | WHEN WAS MY BAG ROASTED? | Tidying the question until it no longer sounds like a person asked it. |
| Replacement | {Cheap thing} instead of {expensive thing}. | A $14 BAG INSTEAD OF DAILY $6 CUPS | Overstating it. If the product replaces part of the expensive thing, say which part. |
| Time | {Long time} to {short time}. | COLD BREW: 24 HOURS TO 4 | A ratio nobody believes, even when it's true. |
| Before and after | {Old thing} / {new thing}. | 3 MONTHS ON A SHELF / 3 DAYS | Changing three things at once. Show one difference. |
| Versus | {A} or {B}? | MONDAY'S ROAST OR A SUPERMARKET BAG? | Refusing to pick. The ad answers its own question. |
| Deadline | {Thing} ends {date}. | THE WINTER BLEND ENDS FRIDAY | Made-up urgency. Use a date the cited page states, or pick another formula. |
| Statistic | {N}% of {group} {fact}. | 90% OF OUR BEANS SHIP IN A DAY | A number the cited page doesn't state. |
