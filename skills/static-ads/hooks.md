# Hook formulas

Each formula is a headline shape, with an example and the formula's trap, the most common way it fails. The examples are for a made-up roaster that roasts every Monday, prints the roast date on each bag, ships within 48 hours of roasting, and sells a light roast at $14 a bag with no subscription needed. Each example assumes its cited page states the fact it uses. The examples are in caps. Set yours in the style guide's headline case.

**Say the number.** When the headline states an amount, a time, or a price, give it as a number. "Fast" and "a lot" are not numbers. Step 3 of SKILL.md builds each idea on an item from the style guide's Only us list.

| Formula | Headline shape | Example | Trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stop | Stop {common habit}. | STOP BUYING BEANS ROASTED BEFORE MONDAY | A habit nobody has. The reader has to recognize it in themselves. |
| Permission | You don't need {thing everyone says you need}. | YOU DON'T NEED A SUBSCRIPTION FOR MONDAY'S ROAST | Taking away the blocker and offering nothing in its place. The sub-line gives the replacement. The cited page has to say the thing isn't needed. |
| Wrong way | {Doing it wrong}? Not your fault. | BEANS OLDER THAN MONDAY? NOT YOUR FAULT | Blaming the reader. Without the absolution, it reads as an insult. |
| Situation | {Specific situation}? | OUT OF BEANS BEFORE MONDAY'S ROAST? | A situation so broad it qualifies nobody. "Want better coffee?" is not a situation. |
| Callout | {Specific group}: {what this is}. | POUR-OVER PEOPLE: MONDAY'S LIGHT ROAST IS YOURS | A group too large to be a callout. "Coffee lovers" is not a group. |
| Objection | "{Objection, in the customer's words}" | "SUBSCRIPTIONS ALWAYS PILE UP" | Inventing the objection. Take it from pasted ads, reviews, or the site's FAQ. |
| Question | {Question as customers type it}? | WHEN WAS MY BAG ROASTED? | Tidying the question until it no longer sounds like a person asked it. |
| Replacement | {Cheap thing} instead of {expensive thing}. | A $14 BAG INSTEAD OF DAILY $6 CUPS | Overstating it. If the product replaces part of the expensive thing, say which part. |
| Time | {Long time} to {short time}. | ROASTER TO DOOR: 3 MONTHS TO 48 HOURS | A ratio nobody believes, even when it's true. |
| Before and after | {Old thing} / {new thing}. | MAY'S BEANS / MONDAY'S BEANS | Changing three things at once. Show one difference. |
| Versus | {A} or {B}? | MONDAY'S ROAST OR A SUPERMARKET BAG? | Refusing to pick. The ad answers its own question. |
| Deadline | {Thing} ends {date}. | THE WINTER BLEND ENDS FRIDAY | Made-up urgency. Use a date the cited page states, or pick another formula. |
| Statistic | {N}% of {group} {fact}. | 90% OF OUR BEANS SHIP IN A DAY | A number the cited page doesn't state. |
| Demonstration | We asked {the product, run on a sample} {the buyer's question, in their words}. | WE ASKED 3 BAGS WHEN THEY WERE ROASTED | Dropping the buyer's question, a speaker who reads as a customer, or a result that reads as the viewer's own. The visual shows the answer, labeled as the sample's. |
